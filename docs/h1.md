# H1: the second series, the scanner, the sessions and the driver

H1 (#667) is the hypothesis that on the broker's synthetic quotes a strong short move continues
over the next seconds. The backtest found it only on the history of the broker's `1s` chart
([signal-stand.md → H1](signal-stand.md#h1-on-the-1s-series-667)), and the effect depends on the
alignment to the second. Since #828 H1 is the only signal: every session the bot starts is an H1
session and every direction the bot shows is H1's. The backend builds a series of second closes
from the live ticks of its data socket; the scanner decides on it for the bot's list and card, and
the driver opens the sessions' trades on it.

The parts: #667 the detector and `stand h1`; #669 the second series and R1's point; #668 the ticks
around a decision and the retention ([trade-log.md → The ticks around a decision](trade-log.md#the-ticks-around-a-decision),
[→ Retention](trade-log.md#retention)); #670 the sessions on H1 (the fence); #707 the driver that
opens their trades; #775 the regular session with H1 entries; #796 the start's reading; #828 the
scanner and H1 for every session; #829 the shown direction; #836 the entry's hold. The shadow of
#669 (the measurement before any money: decisions on every closed second without trading, `h1 shadow report` of #681)
and the account's H1 mark of #670 were removed in #830; their history is in `audits.md` and
`docs/process-decisions.md`, not here.

## The second series

`createSecondSeries` (`apps/backend/src/market-data/seconds.ts`) keeps, per pair, the closes of the
last `H1_SERIES_CAPACITY_SEC` (320) seconds. The market data manager pushes into it every
`price.update` of the active socket's known pairs, next to the candle ring and the tick store, and
calls its `onDrop()` wherever it calls the ring's ([market-data.md](market-data.md)).

- **A second is labelled by its end.** Second `s` (a multiple of 1 000 ms) covers `(s − 1000, s]`,
  as a row of the broker's `1s` chart does: a tick at `t` belongs to `ceil(t / 1000) × 1000`, a
  tick exactly on a boundary to its own second. `c[s]` is the price of the last tick with
  `timestamp ≤ s`, rounded to the pair's `digits` (`Number(price.toFixed(digits))`, the digits of
  the pair's first tick, as the ring's builders), and it is carried over quiet seconds: the
  broker's chart has a row every second, and the probe of 2026-10-20 found the same carry-over.
- **Closing.** Second `f` is closed by its own pair's first tick with `timestamp > f`, at that
  tick's receipt, or by the process clock once `now ≥ f + H1_SECOND_CLOSE_MARGIN_MS` (500 ms),
  whichever comes first; `advance(now)` closes as many seconds as are due, each with its own event,
  in order. The rule is per pair, not the ring's watermark of every pair: another pair's later tick
  does not prove this pair's last tick of `f` has arrived.
- **A closed second never changes.** A tick at or before its pair's last closed second is counted
  (`late`) and not applied, so a decision on `f` and the series it stores agree. An older tick in
  the open second does not replace the newest one; one with the same timestamp received later does.
- **A tick ahead of its receipt.** A tick whose timestamp is more than `H1_SECOND_CLOSE_MARGIN_MS`
  past its receipt is dropped for the series and counted (`ahead`): it would close the seconds up
  to it at once. It does not open a pair either. The tick store takes it and clamps its watermark;
  the ring drops it beyond its own margin `MARKET_DATA_SKEW_MARGIN_MS` (#683,
  [market-data.md → Closing a candle](market-data.md#closing-a-candle-and-holes)).
- **A drop is a hole.** When the active leaves `ready`, every pair's seconds are NaN, not carried
  over, until its first tick after the drop (two drops before it make one hole); from that tick's
  own second on the price is known again. A pair first heard after a drop has no history and no
  hole. The REST backfill does not touch the series: the scanner and the driver read the live stream.
- **A jump past the capacity resets the pair.** More than `H1_SERIES_CAPACITY_SEC` seconds closed
  at once (a stalled process) empties the pair's series without events (`resets`); it warms again.
- **Reading.** `window(assetId, f, n)` gives the closes of the `n` seconds ending at `f` and
  `complete` when each of them is a closed second the series still holds (NaN inside allowed);
  `close`, `lastTickOf` (the tick whose price is `c[f]`: its broker timestamp and receipt),
  `digitsOf`, and `lastClosed(assetId)` (#796): the pair's newest closed second the series holds,
  with how and when it closed (`closedBy`, `closedAtMs`) — undefined before its first close and
  after a reset until the next one. Listeners of `onClosed` run synchronously inside the socket's handler, after the
  state is updated, and must not throw.

Its counters go into the `market data` line as `seconds: { pairs, ahead, late,
closedByTick, closedByClock, holeSeconds, resets }`, per period ([market-data.md → The log](market-data.md#the-log)).

## The row

The H1 rows of `signal_decisions` share one shape (`h1ShadowParamsSchema`, `h1ShadowEntrySchema`,
`packages/shared/src/h1.ts`), one autocommit INSERT each by `recordSignalDecision` (Rule 42), no
UPDATE ([trade-log.md → The table](trade-log.md#the-table)): the driver's rows of a session
(`context = session`, version `h1`; [The driver → The row](#the-driver-707)), next to its
`h1-repeat` and `h1-start` rows and the start's `session_start` row. The shadow's rows
(`context = shadow`, `outcome = shadow`, #669) were written until #830 in the same shape with a
`result` and a `ticks_until_ms`; no one writes them now, and they expire by the shadow band of the
retention, 7 days ([trade-log.md → Retention](trade-log.md#retention)). The columns of an H1 row:

| Column | Value | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| `context`, `outcome` | the driver's: `session` and `intent`, `no_signal` or `refused`; a historical shadow row's: `shadow`, `shadow` | |
| `trading_session_id`, `broker_account_id`, `step` | the driver's: the session, the account, the step; NULL on a shadow row | |
| `interval`, `detail` | NULL | |
| `asset_id`, `payout` | the pair, its catalog payout at the decision (`payoutInColumn`) | |
| `digits` | the digits the series' closes are rounded to (owner, 2026-10-20) | |
| `version` | `h1`; the set is `params.set` (`session` for the driver, `r1`/`h1` on a shadow row), so `decision.version` and the column agree | |
| `decided_at_ms` | `f`, the end label of the closed second decided on | broker |
| `taken_at_ms` | right after the detector | process |
| `recorded_at` | the insert | database |
| `kind`, `action` | `signal`, its direction | |
| `params` | `{ set, moveSec, sdWindowSec, sdStepSec, threshold, entryDelaySec, expirySec }` (`h1ShadowParamsSchema`) | |
| `decision` | the detector's `H1Decision`, with `move`, `sd`, `z`, `moves`, `lastClose` | |
| `source` | `{ kind: 'ticks', symbol, closedBy: 'tick' \| 'clock', closedAtMs }`: what closed `f`, and when | `closedAtMs` process |
| `series` | the `sdWindowSec + 1` closes `c[f − sdWindowSec … f]`, `null` for NaN; the label of `series[i]` is `decided_at_ms − (length − 1 − i) × 1000` | |
| `last_tick` | `{ price, timestamp, receivedAtMs }` of the tick that is `c[f]`; NULL when `c[f]` is NaN | broker, process |

`taken_at_ms − decided_at_ms` is the closing rule's delay (a tick's receipt or `f + 500` plus up to
one interval), not a poll; `last_tick.receivedAtMs` is the receipt of the tick that set `c[f]`, and
`source.closedAtMs` the moment `f` closed. The row carries no token, no `broker_user_id`, no
`telegram_user_id` and no error text. A row is about 2 KB (the series compresses to about 950
bytes, measured on the test database).

## Replay and reading

`trade-log export` ([trade-log.md → The export](trade-log.md#the-export)) gives each row as a line
with its `decision` and `trade` (`null` for a historical shadow row, with its `decision.result`
and `decision.ticksUntilMs`) and the entry under `signal`, shaped by `h1ShadowEntrySchema`
(`packages/shared/src/h1.ts`):

```bash
pnpm --silent --filter @binarius/backend trade-log export --from 2026-10-28T00:00:00Z | grep -c '"version":"h1"'
```

`replayH1Entry(entry)` (`packages/signal/src/h1.ts`) decides the entry again: the series holds the
exact doubles the detector read (rounded at receipt, exact through JSON), with NaN for `null`, at
its last index, so the decision is equal to the stored one bit for bit (`driver.db.test.ts`
H1-DB2′ on the driver's row, `trade-log.db.test.ts` X11 on a shadow row). The stand's
`parseJournalLine` reads such a line as
`{ kind: 'h1', entry }` ([signal-stand.md](signal-stand.md#calibration)).

## R1

The analysis of #667 measured sd as the RMS of the 5-second moves scaled by √(3/5); the detector
takes the RMS of the 3-second moves. R1's step and threshold are the stand's point closest to the
analysis' 321 trades/pair-h at +1 s on the 7 pairs ≥ 94: sd step 5, threshold 1, 311.2 trades/pair-h
at 58.77 % (58.5 % there). The run and its table: [signal-stand.md → R1's point](signal-stand.md#h1-on-the-1s-series-667).
`H1_R1_PARAMS` lives in `packages/shared/src/h1.ts`, passes `assertH1Params` at the import of
`packages/signal`, and its values are in the stand's grids (`config.test.ts` K6).

## Start and stop

`apps/backend/src/index.ts` builds the series with the candle ring, only with `BROKER_WS_URL`, and
the driver and the scanner on it with the backfill and the manager; they start after `listen()` and
stop first in shutdown phase 1 — the scanner, then the driver — before the backfill and the

left in `.env` changes nothing (compose no longer forwards it).

## Sessions (#670)

Part 3a: the fence of a session that trades on H1 — the settings, the deadline of a submit and
the stops. Part 3b (#707) adds [the driver](#the-driver-707) that opens the trades:
the orchestrator only holds an H1 session and stops it.

**The session.** Settings v5 since #775 ([trading-session.md → Settings](trading-session.md#settings)):
a regular session - its cycles, the stake strategy of the process (`SESSION_STAKE_STRATEGY`,
martingale by default with `martingaleLimitsFor`), the user's saved stake of the mode or the
broker's minimum - plus R1's parameters, entry +1 s and expiry 5 s. Only the entries differ: they
come from H1 ([The driver](#the-driver-707)). Settings v4 of #670 (a fixed stake, N trades,
`maxLoss`) are read-only: nothing writes them, the rows on the pilot still parse, and their
`maxLoss` is no longer enforced.

```bash
docker compose exec -T backend pnpm --silent --filter @binarius/backend h1 session start --account <accountId> --asset <pairId> --cycles 5
docker compose exec -T backend pnpm --silent --filter @binarius/backend h1 session status --account <accountId>
docker compose exec -T backend pnpm --silent --filter @binarius/backend h1 session stop --account <accountId>
```

`session start` checks, in this order, each with its own line on stderr and exit 1 before any
write: the account exists; `DEMO_ONLY=true` with the user in real mode (before any
broker read, Rule 33); one fresh read of the broker's pairs (the CLI's own GET to
`BROKER_API_BASE_URL`, outside the backend's pacers); the pair is listed, open and takes 5 s (no
payout floor since #775, В2); the session fits its hour in its strategy's worst case
(`sessionFitsDeadline(cycles, 5, strategy)`: `сессия не укладывается в час: уменьшите --cycles`,
more than 7 cycles under martingale); `checkTradingSessionStart` (the switch, the user, the
account, an active session — its id printed — and the tokens); the balance snapshot (none: open
the trade screen in the bot); the stake — the user's saved stake of the mode, else the broker's
minimum — against that mode's balance (`checkStake`); the settings. `--cycles` is 1 – 20, 5 by
default (`DEFAULT_SESSION_CYCLES`); the strategy is the process's `SESSION_STAKE_STRATEGY`, read as
the backend reads it (Rule 14). The session's mode is the
user's `trading_mode` (Rule 36). stdout is the session's id; a failure after the commit may have
lost its answer, and the line then names the query that re-reads the account's sessions. The
account's H1 mark was removed in #830 (migration 0085, with `h1 mark|unmark|list`): the CLI starts
a session on any account, and any other command is a usage error (exit 2).
`session stop` stops the account's active session only when it is an H1 one (a bot session is the
bot's), with `user_stopped`; `session status` prints the newest H1 session of the account (the
active one, else the latest started): status, stop reason, mode, the cycles closed of planned, the
settled trades with the wins, losses, ties and rejections, the profit, the start and the end. No
H1 session is exit 1.

**The stops.** The orchestrator ([trading-session.md → The orchestrator](trading-session.md#the-orchestrator)):
as a regular session's (#775): `completed` after its cycles, the sizer's limits
(`max_steps_exceeded`, `max_stake_exceeded`, `max_session_loss_exceeded`, `stake_stop`), the hour
(`timeout`), a closed pair past the no-signal limit (`no_signal_timeout`), `/stop`, the «⏹» and
`h1 session stop` (`user_stopped`) and the technical ones. The payout is not read. The no-signal
stop itself (В5) is the driver's ([The no-signal limit](#the-driver-707)): `no_signal_timeout`
after 100 seconds (`SESSION_NO_SIGNAL_CANDLES` × 5 s) on which the driver decided `no_signal` since
the session last changed, with the database's wait (`waitExpired`) expired too; the count is in
the backend's memory and restarts with it.

**The deadline of a submit.** `trade_intents.submit_deadline_at` (NULL — no window) is written only
on a session's path (`createSessionIntent` → `IntentSession.submitDeadlineAt`; the intent route's
request has no such field). The worker's `takeIntent` refuses an intent past it on the database
clock in the same CAS as its age and the switch, and `rejectLateIntent` makes it `rejected` with
`entry_window_missed`, the reserve released; nothing reaches the broker
([trade-intent-transport.md](trade-intent-transport.md#delivery-ack-and-timeout-semantics)). The
driver (#707) is its one writer in production: `max(submit_not_before_at, taken_at_ms) +
H1_SUBMIT_BUDGET_MS`, the decision row's `taken_at_ms` ([The entry](#the-entry-836)). `trade_intents.submit_not_before_at` (#836,
NULL — no hold) is written on the same path (`IntentSession.submitNotBeforeAt`): the worker does
not take the intent before it; CHECK `trade_intents_submit_window_check` keeps it before the
deadline when both are set.

Deploy order (Rule 28): `trading-worker` first (it reads v4 and the take's predicate), then
`backend`, `bot` and `web` together; migration 0073 runs at the first container's start.

#### The start (#796)

Before #796 the start checked the candle signal (v2), showed its direction and traded nothing on
it: the first trade waited for the driver's next H1 signal (the pilot, 2026-10-24: start → first
open 9.4 s median on H1 against 3.6 s on v3, 7 of 10 first trades against the shown direction). Now
the start reads H1 itself and hands its signal to the driver:

- **The reading** (`H1Driver.current(assetId, settings)`): taken last, right after the v5 settings
  and right before the insert, since an H1 signal lives one second. `f` is
  `series.lastClosed(assetId)`, the newest closed second; the reading is current only while
  `now − f ≤ H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS` (1 600 ms: a second plus the clock close's margin and one
  tick, the latest a quiet pair's next second closes). Then the session's own detector
  (`settings.signal.params`, never `H1_R1_PARAMS` directly) decides on the window of
  `sdWindowSec + 1` closes ending at `f`, exactly as the driver's closed-second path does; it
  touches no session and no counter. Without a reading — the source down (or no data socket at
  all: `BROKER_WS_URL` unset), the pair never ticked, a stale `f` (a stalled event loop), unknown
  digits, a window still warming — one `info` `h1 start without a reading { accountId, assetId,
  reason }`.
- **With a signal**: `start_signal_action` is its direction, the `session_start` row `started`
  (version `h1`), and `adopt(session, { facts, pair })` runs the driver's own trade on the start's
  second: the history, `sessionIntentBlock`, the balance, the sizer, `createSessionIntent` with
  the reading's second's hold and deadline ([The entry](#the-entry-836): `submit_not_before_at =
  closedAtMs + entryDelaySec`, the deadline from the later of the hold and the reading's
  `taken_at_ms`; a late
  take is `entry_window_missed`, as for any H1 signal), its `intent` row of step 1, the wake. The driver
  stays the only creator of H1 intents (Rule 48); the route never calls `createSessionIntent`.
- **Without a signal** (owner, 2026-10-24: no refusal): the session starts and waits;
  `start_signal_action` NULL, the bot's session message shows «📡 Ждём сигнал…»
  ([bot-session.md](bot-session.md)), the `session_start` row `no_signal` with the session's id.
  A counted `no_signal` (`below_threshold`, `no_volatility`) is the session's first counted second:
  the driver writes the waiting row at once, so «🔎 Ждём сигнал для следующей сделки…» comes on the
  sender's next tick. Without a reading the session is adopted without facts and the next closed
  second decides.
- **The hand-over** (`adopt`): fire-and-forget, never throws (`errors`, warn `h1 driver adopt
  failed`). A session a scan already tracks keeps its state (a second before its next one is
  `held`, a run in flight `skippedLive`); a scan whose list began before the hand-over does not drop
  it; a stopped driver (shutdown) ignores it, and the next process's first scan adopts the session.

The start's open lands at `f + 0.3…2.0 s` (the reading's age plus ~0.3 s of path), inside the
backtest's +1/+2 s entry window (`H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS < H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC.to × 1000`). The
CLI `h1 session start` is unchanged: no reading, adopted by the next scan.

What the user sees: the regular session ([bot-session.md](bot-session.md)): the session message
without a button, the trade blocks «⚡ Цикл N Шаг M» in the cycle message, the open trade's block
with «⏹», «🔎 Ждём сигнал…» with «⏹» between trades, the completed line «🏁 Сессия завершена:
5 циклов, …» and the summary. A start the bot made from its list or a card goes on the direction it
showed ([The shown direction](#the-shown-direction-829)); a start without such a record on a second
without an H1 signal begins «📡 Ждём сигнал…» and the first trade comes with the driver's first
signal, seconds later.

#### The shown direction (#829)

The owner's decisions 4 and 5 (#828): the first trade and the session's picture are the direction
the bot showed when the pair was chosen — the list's button or the analysis card — even when the
pair's signal is gone by the press, and that direction has no expiry.

- **The record** is the bot's ([bot-demo.md](bot-demo.md)): `(telegramUserId, assetId) → { action,
  second, decidedAt, source: list | analysis, shownAtMs }` in the bot's memory, written once a screen
  with directions went out (the list, the wait's redraws, the card), from the backend's answers
  (`GET /trading/signals` items, `POST /trading/signal`'s `h1`) — never from callback data. The
  start takes it once and sends it as `shownSignal` of `POST /trading/sessions`
  (`shownSignalSchema`, strict: `action`, positive integer `second`, `decidedAt`, `shownAtMs`,
  `source` of `ShownSignalSource`).
- **The route** with `shownSignal`: after the v5 settings no `current()` call; one `info` `h1 start
  on a shown signal { accountId, assetId, source, ageMs }` (`ageMs` the route's clock less
  `second`); `start_signal_action = shownSignal.action`; `createTradingSession`; `adopt(session, {
  shown, pair })`; the `session_start` row of version `h1-start` (`started` with the session;
  `refused` with the wire code and no session when the insert refuses); 201. No age check
  (decision 5): the trusted internal bearer (Rule 13) carries the bot's echo of the backend's own
  answer. Without the driver (no data socket) the session starts in that direction and nothing
  adopts it, as a start without a reading.
- **The driver's plan `shown`** (`adopt` with `{ shown, pair }`): a run in flight — `skippedLive`;
  before the session's next second (`now() < nextAt`) — `held`; else the trade, with the repeat rule
  first as always: the shown direction, the sizer's stake over the start's pair's payout, the
  entry's hold from the shown decision (`submit_not_before_at = decidedAt + entryDelaySec`, #836;
  usually behind at the press) and **no submit window** (decision 5: the window protects the entry
  +1…+2 s of a live reading; a shown direction is traded at any age), its `intent` row of version
  `h1-start` in the intent's transaction, the next decision at its creation + `expirySec + 1` s, a wake, `shown` counted. Refusals, replays and
  the gone session end as any step.
- **The rows** (`H1_START_VERSION`): `kind = signal`, the shown `action`, `decided_at_ms = second`
  of the shown signal, `taken_at_ms` each writer's clock, the payout of the start's catalog read,
  `params = { rule: shown_signal, …H1_SCANNER_PARAMS }` (the detector the shown decision was made
  with), `decision = { kind: signal, version: h1-start, action, shown: { second, decidedAt, source }
  }`, `source = { kind: shown, source, shownAtMs }`, `series = []`, `digits`, `last_tick` and
  `ticks_until_ms` NULL. `taken_at_ms − decided_at_ms` of the start row is the shown direction's

## The scanner and the signals list (#828)

`apps/backend/src/h1/scanner.ts` (`createH1Scanner`) is what the bot shows (decision 2): the signals
list (`GET /trading/signals`) and the analysis's direction (`POST /trading/signal` → `h1`). It is
built in `createMarketData` only with `BROKER_WS_URL`, listens to the same second series as the
driver and advances nothing (the driver's tick closes quiet seconds). On every
closed second `f` of every pair of the fresh catalog that is open and takes `H1_EXPIRY_SEC` — no
payout floor (decision 8) — while the source streams, `createH1Detector(H1_SCANNER_PARAMS)`
(`H1_R1_PARAMS`, the source `buildH1SessionSettings` writes: the shown signal and the traded one
are the same function of the same second) decides on the window of `sdWindowSec + 1` closes at
`at = length − 1` (Rule 43). The pair's newest decision is kept in memory, nothing is written.

A decision is served only while its second is current: `now − f ≤ H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS`
(1 600 ms, the start's rule of #796: a healthy series always has a current second) and only when
it is a signal. `fresh(now)` gives the list, `scanned` (the pairs with a current decision) and the
summary of the same current decisions (#859): `streaming`, `scanning` (the current pairs by
`assetId`, the one at `floor(now / 1000) mod scanned`, null without one) and `closeness` (each
current `below_threshold` pair's `h1ClosenessPct(z, threshold)` = `min(99, floor(100 · z /
threshold))`, highest first, then by `assetId`; a signal and `no_price`/`no_volatility` carry none);
`current(assetId, now)` one pair's signal or null. The session notices' sender reads `fresh` too, in
its own process, for the session pair's lines in the waiting message's caption (#860,
[bot-session.md → The waiting message](bot-session.md#the-waiting-message-536)). The entries (one per pair; a pair's newest
decision replaces the previous one; the map is a snapshot, not a queue):

| An entry's second ends by | Entry | Served |
|---|---|---|
| the pair's next closed second (tick or clock), eligible, source up | replaced by the new decision | while current (≤ 1 600 ms old) and a signal |
| the next closed second with the source down | every entry deleted (`sourceDown`) | nothing |
| the next closed second with a stale catalog | kept; `catalogStale`, one `warn` per streak | until it ages out |
| the pair not in the catalog, closed, or not taking 5 s at its next second | its entry deleted (`ineligible`) | nothing |
| the window incomplete (warm-up, a reset) | its entry deleted (`warming`) | nothing |
| the pair's seconds stop closing (only a reset: the clock closes quiet seconds) | kept until the next close | ages out |
| `stop()` | unsubscribed, the map cleared | nothing |

A throw inside the decision is counted (`errors`) and warned `h1 scanner decision failed`; the
socket's handler never sees it. After a drop of the source the entries clear at the first closed
second, and the first ~300 s after the reconnect are `warming` (the window): the list stays empty
for five minutes, as the driver's warm-up.

**The wire.** `GET /trading/signals` keeps its frame — `lists[]` with one list keyed
`intervalForDuration(H1_EXPIRY_SEC)` (`5s`, the list of the bot's one duration) — with the item
`{ assetId, action, second, decidedAt, ageMs }` (`second` is `f`; the candle item's
`lastCandleTimestamp` is gone) and the list's `summary` (#859, the bot's wait screen); without a
scanner (no data socket) `scanned: 0`, no signals and the empty summary (`EMPTY_H1_SCANNER_LIST`).
`POST /trading/signal` answers `h1: { action, second, decidedAt, ageMs } | null` on both outcomes,
read right after the candle feed answered (the feed may take up to `SIGNAL_FETCH_BUDGET_MS`); the
analysis reminder (#484) follows `h1`, on `fetch_failed` too. The bot ([bot-demo.md](bot-demo.md)):
the card's direction, picture, title, «➕ Ещё» and session row come from `h1` only; the candle
decision prints the card's rows (decision 3: information only); without `h1` the analysis waits.

**The line** `h1 scanner`, every `H1_SCANNER_LOG_MS`: `pairs` (entries), `current`, `listed`,
then totals since start `decisions`, `signals`, `noSignal` by reason, `warming`, `ineligible`,
`sourceDown`, `catalogStale`, `errors`, and the period's `lagMsP50`/`lagMsP95` (`takenAtMs − f`).
The start line: `h1 scanner { enabled, maxAgeMs }`. The candle scanner (#343) and its `signal
scanner` line are gone.

**The load** (the pilot: 1 vCPU, 961 MB): 144 decisions a second on the series' closes, 0.92 ms of

## The driver (#707)

`apps/backend/src/h1/driver.ts` (`createH1Driver`) is the only creator of an H1 session's intents
(Rule 48). It listens to the same second series as the scanner and reads the same catalog; the
payout is never a gate (owner 2026-10-19: a payout that falls during the session does not stop it;
#775: none at the start either), only the sizer's input. Since #775 it sizes every intent with the
session's sizer (`@binarius/stake`, the orchestrator's) over the session's history and balance,
creates the repeat step, records the waiting periods and stops a session at the no-signal limit.

**The sessions.** Every `H1_DRIVER_SCAN_MS` (5 s) `listActiveH1Sessions` reads the active H1
sessions (v5, or a read-only v4 row) of any account (since #828), oldest start first. A new one is parsed once, and its sizer built (`createStakeSizer(sessionStakeParams(settings))`):
settings that are not H1's, a stake the sizer refuses, or R1 parameters whose window does not fit (`assertH1WindowFits`,
`moveSec <= sdStepSec`), give one warn `h1 driver session skipped
{ reason: invalidSettings }` and no decision; the orchestrator stops a non-H1 row and a stake the
sizer refuses `invalid_settings` (#835); a window that does not fit is held until its hour. A session no longer listed is dropped; one the driver stopped, or whose row is gone or no
longer active, is forgotten until a scan no longer lists it. The bot's start hands its new session
over at once (`adopt`, #796, [The start](#the-start-796)), so it does not wait for the next scan.

**The second.** On a closed second `f` of a session's pair, in this order: no session on the pair
— nothing; the data socket not streaming — `sourceDown`; the catalog not fresh — `catalogStale`
(one warn per stale run); the pair not listed, closed by its schedule or not taking 5 s —
`ineligible`. Then per session: a run in flight — `skippedLive`; a pending repeat step — its retry
(below), no decision, no digits needed (its row has none); `f` before the session's next second —
`held`; the pair's digits not known yet — nothing; the window of `sdWindowSec + 1` closes not
complete — `warming`; else the session's detector decides (`decisions`, the lag `taken_at_ms − f`);
a `no_signal` counts by its reason, and unless it is `no_price` (a hole in the data, not a reading
of the market) it adds a second to the session's no-signal count and may start the no-signal run
(below); a signal starts the trade. None of the skips adds to the count.

**The trade** (asynchronous, one per session at a time). One read of the session's history and
balance, `readSessionBatch` for this one session (#874: two statements, the orchestrator's own
read, where it was three), then
`sessionIntentBlock` (`packages/shared/src/trading-session.ts`), the function the orchestrator's
decision reads too: the hour passed, a live intent of the session, the cycles completed, the last
two intents rejected (an `entry_window_missed` among them) — each a skip (`skippedLive`,
`skippedLimits`), the stop is the orchestrator's within its next attempt. Then the repeat rule
(`repeatDue`, below): when it holds, this step is the repeat, whatever started the run. Then the
session's balance (from the same read, with `awaitsBalance`; none —
`skippedLimits.balance_unavailable`) and the sizer over `sessionStakeInput` (the orchestrator's
input: `sessionTradeOf` of each intent, the payout, the snapshot's minimum and available, the clock
held at `started_at`): a stop is a skip
counted by its reason (`max_steps_exceeded`, `max_stake_exceeded`, `max_session_loss_exceeded`, …)
— the orchestrator stops the session on the same reading; `insufficient_balance` while a settlement
is newer than the balance (#696) is a hold (`heldBalance`); a stake is the intent's amount.
Otherwise `createSessionIntent` with step = the session's intents + 1, the sizer's stake, the
signal's direction, 5 s, `submitNotBeforeAt = closedAtMs + entryDelaySec` and `submitDeadlineAt =
max(submitNotBeforeAt, the signal's takenAtMs) + H1_SUBMIT_BUDGET_MS` (the backend's clock, [The
entry](#the-entry-836)) and its decision row in the intent's transaction; after the commit,
`publisher.wake()`. The next second the session may decide is `f + (entryDelaySec + expirySec + 1)` s — 7 s for R1, the
one-trade-at-a-time cadence of #669. Two H1 sessions on one pair (two accounts) decide independently.

**The repeat step (#775, В1).** Шаг 2 of a cycle - the trade after a settled Шаг 1 that lost or
tied, under martingale - is not waited for: `repeatDue(history, settings)` holds when the session's
last intent is settled, not a win, and is Шаг 1 by `sessionTradeSteps` (the numbering of the
session's messages); under fixed never. Its trigger is the settlement's notification:
`transitionIntent` notifies `SESSION_NOTICE_CHANNEL` with the session's id on `accepted` and
`settled` (#723, at COMMIT), and the backend's listener hands it to the sender and to
`h1Driver.notice(sessionId)`, which runs the trade without a signal (a notice while a run is in
flight runs it once more after). The step goes in the lost trade's direction, at the sizer's stake
(the raise; after a tie the base stake), with no hold and no submit window (as a regular
session's Шаг 2: the entry is H1's; it has no signal second whose pullback the hold waits out),
and its own row (below); the next decision of the session is no sooner than
its creation + `expirySec + 1` s. A run after a win or after a lost Шаг 2 creates nothing
(`noticeIdle`); Шаг 1 of a cycle and Шаг 3 and on wait for an H1 signal and take its direction. A
notification the listener lost (its reconnect) is caught by the next H1 signal, whose run checks
`repeatDue` first (`source.trigger = signal`). A repeat held for the balance or by an ineligible
pair is pending (`repeatPending`): it is tried again on every closed second of the pair until it is
created or no longer due (`source.trigger = tick`).

**The waiting row (#775, В3).** A counted `no_signal` second (`below_threshold` or
`no_volatility`) of a session with no waiting row yet starts the no-signal run: the history is read;
a block (a live intent, the limits) writes nothing; a due repeat step (`repeatDue`, after a lost
notice or a restart) writes nothing either and marks the repeat pending (`repeatPending`), so Шаг 2
goes on the next closed second; otherwise one `signal_decisions` row is written, `kind = no_signal`,
`outcome = no_signal`, the detector's decision and the window's series, `step` the next one
(`waitingRows`). A `no_price` second opens no waiting period. It is the row `waitingForSignalSql`
reads, so the sender's waiting message «🔎 Ждём сигнал…» with its «⏹», its delete when the next
intent's row lands and the stop route's hand-over (#587) work for an H1 session as for any. One row
per waiting period: the next one opens after the session's next intent or notice; a refused INSERT
(`failed`) is tried again on a counted second after `scanMs`.

**The no-signal limit (#775, review round 1).** The orchestrator's attempt never asks H1, so it has
no evidence that a signal was looked for; the driver has. `noSignalSeconds` counts the session's
counted seconds (above) on the broker's seconds, cumulatively (a gap pauses it); every intent the
driver creates or replays, every notice and `add()` reset it, so a backend restart counts from
zero. The limit is `sessionNoSignalTimeoutMs(durationSec) / 1000`, 100 for 5 s, from the worker's
`SESSION_NO_SIGNAL_CANDLES`. On a counted second at or past it the no-signal run reads the history
and, in this order: a block — nothing; a due repeat — pending, as above; no waiting row yet — the
waiting row first (so the user sees «🔎 Ждём сигнал…» before «сигнала не было»); `waitExpired`
false (the database's clock since `greatest(started_at, max(updated_at))`) — nothing; else
`stopTradingSession` with `no_signal_timeout` (`noSignalStops`), then the stopping second's
`no_signal` row, best-effort, whether the CAS matched or not. The seconds the driver cannot decide
— the source down, a stale catalog, an ineligible pair, no digits, an incomplete window (the warm-up
after a restart or the pair's first tick), `no_price`, a run in flight, the cadence, a pending
repeat — do not count. A closed pair past the limit stays the orchestrator's.

### The entry (#836)

The backtest and the shadow entered `entryDelaySec` (1 s, R1) after the closed second `f`; until
#836 the driver sent at once and the broker opened in the median 0.33 s after `f`, inside the
pullback after the move (on the pilot's ticks: 46.5 % at `f + 0`, 54.7 % at `f + 1 s`). Since #836
no H1 intent is taken before its second + `entryDelaySec`:

| Value | What | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| `f` | the decided second's end (`decided_at_ms`) | broker |
| `closedAtMs` | when the series closed `f` (`source.closedAtMs`): the first tick past `f`, or the process clock at `f + 500` (the driver's next tick, up to 100 ms later) | backend |
| `submit_not_before_at` | `closedAtMs + entryDelaySec × 1000` (the shown direction: `shown.decidedAt + …`, the scanner's clock, the same backend's) | backend |
| `submit_deadline_at` | `max(submit_not_before_at, taken_at_ms) + H1_SUBMIT_BUDGET_MS`, `taken_at_ms` the decision row's (the second's decision, or the start's reading), a signal's only | backend |
| the hold | `min(INTENT_HOLD_MAX_MS, submit_not_before_at − now)` when above 0, before `takeIntent`, after the forward to the owner (#347) | worker |
| `open_timestamp_ms` | the broker's open: the target `open − f` in `[1000, 2000]`, none below 1000 | broker |

`closedAtMs`, not `f`: a tick-closed second's `closedAtMs` is the receipt of a tick past `f`, so a
send at `closedAtMs + 1000` on the backend's clock is at least `f + 1000` on the broker's whatever
their offset; a clock-closed second (`f + 500`) holds the bound while the broker lags the backend
by at most 500 ms, the series' own condition (its tick is `late` otherwise: [Accepted
risks](#accepted-risks) 1). `taken_at_ms` would not do: a start's
reading is up to 1.6 s old. The hold is the worker's, not a delayed creation: the delivery backend →
Redis → worker spends the time before the bound, not the budget after it. The budget runs from
the later of the hold's end and the decision's `taken_at_ms`, never from the creation: the
creation comes after the history and balance reads, and a slow one would carry the take past
`+2 s` (#836, review round 1). An old reading (#796) whose bound is already behind keeps the
budget from the reading, as before #836.

| Path | `submit_not_before_at` | `submit_deadline_at` |
|---|---|---|
| a live signal of a closed second | `closedAtMs + entryDelaySec` | from the hold's end |
| the start's reading (#796) | the same, of the reading's second | from the later of the hold and the reading's `taken_at_ms` |
| the shown direction (#829) | `shown.decidedAt + entryDelaySec` | none (decision 5) |
| the repeat step (`h1-repeat`) | none | none |
| `POST /trading/intents`, a regular session | none | none |

The worker (`apps/trading-worker/src/intents/processor.ts`): `DEMO_ONLY` → the forward → the hold
→ `takeIntent` (the age, the deadline and the switch on the database clock, after the hold: a
switch closed during it rejects `trading_paused`, nothing reaches the broker) → the submit. One
`info` `intent held for its entry { intentId, holdMs }` per hold; a bound more than
`INTENT_HOLD_MAX_MS` (2 s) ahead is a wrong row, held 2 s with a `warn` `intent hold clamped
{ intentId, wantedMs, holdMs }`. A worker that dies in the hold has the job redelivered after its
lock: the bound is behind, the deadline too — `entry_window_missed`, as for a death before the take
until now. Deploy: `trading-worker` before `backend`; an old worker ignores the column (opens early
until it is updated), a new worker with an old backend sees NULL (no hold). Tests: `driver.test.ts`
H1-DR1, DR40, DR45, DR51–DR55, `driver.db.test.ts` H1-DB1, DB8, `session-routes.db.test.ts` H1-R10,
H1-R17, `processor.db.test.ts` H1-L6–L10, `schema.db.test.ts` H1-D3,

**Start and stop.** `index.ts` builds the driver with the scanner, only with `BROKER_WS_URL`
(without it an H1 session trades nothing and stops at its hour); there is no switch: with no active
H1 session it only listens. It starts after `listen()`, scans at once, and its tick
(`H1_SERIES_TICK_MS`, 100 ms) calls `series.advance()`: it is the series' clock, the one that
closes quiet seconds (`advance` is idempotent). In shutdown phase 1 it stops after the scanner,
before the backfill and the manager: the timers and the subscription end and `stop()` waits for the
creations in flight (ordinary database latency); no wake after it. The start line `h1 driver

**`H1_SUBMIT_BUDGET_MS`** (`apps/backend/src/env.ts`, Rule 14): 100–1000, absent is 700, empty or
out of range fails the start. Only the backend reads it (compose forwards it to `backend` without a
value, outside the broker anchor). It bounds only the wait for the worker's take: a take after
`submit_deadline_at` (the database's clock) rejects the intent `entry_window_missed` without a
broker call; after the take the submit runs under the worker's own timeouts. It runs from the
entry's hold's end (#836), and its ceiling is the entry window's width (`H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC`,
1 s): a take at the budget's end comes at most 1 s after the hold's end. The hold ends at the
second's close + `entryDelaySec`, and the close comes after `f` (a clock close at `f + 500`, up to
one 100 ms tick later), so a live signal's take may come up to about `f + 2.3 s` at 700 and
`f + 2.6 s` at the ceiling; a start on an old reading later still (the driver's accepted risk 18).
After a change: `docker compose up -d backend`.

   step 4), or `entry_window_missed` above 2 % of the sessions' intents with a healthy worker.
2. **Two clocks for the deadline** (the backend's writes it, the database's compares it): one host
   on the pilot. Falsifiable: `entry_window_missed` above 50 % at 700 with a healthy worker.
3. **An H1 session on any account**: traded since #828, as every session the bot starts (until
   then only on a marked account; the mark is gone with #830).
4. **Scale**: up to 20 H1 sessions on the pilot since #828 (every session is H1's); per signal one
   history read, one balance read and the intent's transaction; per settlement notice one history
   read (two with `accepted`); per waiting period one history read and one INSERT; per scan one
   SELECT. Falsifiable: `lagMsP95` of the line above 1 000 ms at 20 sessions.
5. **The repeat rides on the LISTEN notification** (#775): a notification lost in the listener's
   reconnect delays Шаг 2 to the next H1 signal (seconds; the direction is still the lost trade's).
   Falsifiable: repeat rows with `source.trigger = signal` above 5 % of the repeat rows in a
   trading day.
6. **Volume**: about 300 decision rows an hour per session at about 2 KB (301 closes) and the
   keeper's window of ticks for each, kept with their intent (Rule 45). Falsifiable:
   `signal_decisions` growing more than 10 MB in an H1 session's hour.
7. **Settings whose window does not fit** (by hand): the session does not trade until its hour.
8. **One backend process**: a second driver would make the same steps — a replay by the step's key,
   never a second intent (`trade_intents_active_account_idx`), but its decisions twice.
9. **The `refused` row is best-effort** (as the orchestrator's was until #835).
10. **Two sizers over one history** (#775): the driver's and the orchestrator's reads are not one
    transaction; a settlement between them changes the orchestrator's stake, never the driver's
    created intent - the orchestrator then stops at its next attempt (≤ 5 s), as for a regular
    session's intent created a tick earlier.
11. **A v4 session active at the deploy loses its `maxLoss`** (#775): the sizer's fixed strategy
    has no ceiling; the owner stops any active H1 session before the deploy. Nothing writes v4.
12. **The repeat has no submit window and no hold**: a worker restart between the notice and the
    take opens Шаг 2 later than «at once» - a regular session's Шаг 2 has no window either.
13. **One extra `no_signal` row per backend restart** (the driver forgets its waiting periods):
    the sender claims the waiting message once per slot.
14. **The backend down, or the source down, all hour** (#775, review round 1): the driver decides
    nothing, so neither process stops the session for no signal; it ends with `timeout` at its
    hour, with no waiting message. A regular session whose signal reads fail all hour does the
    same. Falsifiable: an H1 session stopped `timeout` with zero `context = session` rows.
15. **A restart lengthens the wait**: the count restarts at zero, so a session waits up to the
    warm-up (about 300 s) plus 100 s past its last change - longer than before the restart, never
    shorter. Falsifiable: an H1 `no_signal_timeout` whose stopping row's `decided_at_ms` is less
    than 99 s after the first `no_signal` row of that period.
16. **The cadence `f + 7 s` is not moved by the hold** (#836): the open at about `f + 1.2 s`, the
    close at `f + 6.2 s`, the settlement at `f + 6.3…6.6 s` leave about 0.7 s before the next
    decision instead of 1.7 s; a late settlement is one `skippedLive` second. Falsifiable:
    `skippedLive` above 10 % of `signals` in an `h1 driver` line of a trading hour; then an issue on
    `entryDelaySec + expirySec + 2`.
17. **The hold occupies a worker slot** (`WORKER_CONCURRENCY` 5): at most 1 s per H1 trade.
    Falsifiable: `entry_window_missed` growing with the `intent held for its entry` lines at 20
    sessions; then `WORKER_CONCURRENCY` in `.env`.
18. **A start on an old reading opens at the window's tail** (#836): its bound may be behind the
    reading, the deadline runs from the reading's `taken_at_ms` (as before #836), so the open may
    land after `f + 2 s`; so may a live signal on a clock-closed second (`f + 500…600 ms`: the
    take by about `f + 2.3 s` at 700); the p90 of owner's step 4 measures both. **A press on the
    waiting screen** within a second of the shown decision waits up to 1 s for Шаг 1 (#829
    promised «within a second or two»).

| Constant | Value | Bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `H1_SECOND_CLOSE_MARGIN_MS` | 500 | a quiet pair's second closes by the clock this long after its end |
| `H1_SERIES_TICK_MS` | 100 | the series' clock rule: the driver's tick, `series.advance()` |
| `H1_SERIES_CAPACITY_SEC` | 320 | seconds per pair |

| `H1_DRIVER_SCAN_MS` | 5 000 | the driver's read of the active H1 sessions |
| `H1_DRIVER_LOG_MS` | 60 000 | the `h1 driver` line |

| `H1_SCANNER_LOG_MS` | 60 000 | the `h1 scanner` line (#828) |

The links: `TICK ≤ MARGIN` (a clock close lags its rule by at most one tick); `MARGIN <
MARKET_DATA_SKEW_MARGIN_MS` (a second closes before the ring's clock rule would close its bucket)
and `< TICK_MAX_SKEW_MS`; `CAPACITY ≥ H1_SHADOW_MAX_WINDOW_SEC + H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC.to +
H1_EXPIRY_SEC + 1` (320 ≥ 308: the longest window a reader may take, the sets' ceiling, and the
latest exit). Memory: 144
pairs × 320 seconds × three arrays of doubles, about 1 MB. The driver's: `MIN ≤ DEFAULT ≤ MAX` and
`H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC.from × 1000 + MAX_H1_SUBMIT_BUDGET_MS ≤ H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC.to × 1000` (#836: the
budget is at most the window's width after the hold's end; the close's lag past `f` comes on top,
[The entry](#the-entry-836)); the worker's `INTENT_HOLD_MAX_MS
= H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC.to × 1000` (2 000) sits in its shutdown chain
([trade-intent-transport.md](trade-intent-transport.md#delivery-ack-and-timeout-semantics)); `H1_SERIES_TICK_MS ≤ H1_DRIVER_SCAN_MS <
SHUTDOWN_PHASE1_BUDGET_MS`; `H1_DRIVER_SCAN_MS ≤ H1_DRIVER_LOG_MS`. The start's (#796): `1000 + H1_SECOND_CLOSE_MARGIN_MS +
H1_SERIES_TICK_MS ≤ H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS` (a healthy series always has a current second) and
`H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS < H1_ENTRY_WINDOW_SEC.to × 1000` (a current reading opens inside the
entry window). The scanner's (#828): `H1_SERIES_TICK_MS ≤ H1_SCANNER_LOG_MS`. The bot's wait
(`apps/bot/src/timing.ts`): `H1_SECOND_CLOSE_MARGIN_MS < SIGNAL_WAIT_POLL_OFFSET_MS (600) <
SIGNAL_WAIT_POLL_MS (1 000) ≤ H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS − H1_SECOND_CLOSE_MARGIN_MS` (a second
closed at the latest by the clock rule is still served at the next poll).

## Accepted risks

1. **The clock margin (500 ms) is a quarter of the ring's**: a tick delayed past it misses its quiet
   second. Falsifiable: `seconds.late` above 1 % of the closed seconds in a trading hour.
2. **The entry is `c[f + 1]`**, as in the backtest; a live order of #670 opens later by
   `taken_at_ms − f` plus the order's delay, which the rows measure.
3. **The REST backfill does not repair the series**: a hole stays NaN (`no_price`).
4. One backend process; clocks printed raw (broker, process, database); the decisions run inside
   the socket's handler (the scanner's detector per eligible pair and second, the driver's per
   session on the pair).
5. **A broker clock ahead of the process by more than 500 ms minus the receipt delay** drops every
   tick of the series: the scanner and the driver stay `warming`. A tick less than 500 ms ahead closes its previous
   second at most one margin before the process clock would, and that second's later ticks go to
   `late`. Falsifiable: `seconds.ahead` above 0 in the line. The ring, with its 2 s margin, keeps
   streaming through such a skew.
6. **The scanner's list is a snapshot of one second and flips on every refresh** (#828, decision 2
   with a 1 s signal): about a quarter of the eligible pairs at a time, a different quarter a
   second later. A second whose decision the socket's handler could not compute is a gap, not a
   stale answer. Falsifiable: the owner asks for a hold; then a separate served-age constant, not
   `H1_START_SECOND_MAX_AGE_MS`, is the knob.
7. **One poll a second from the bot while anyone waits** (#828): `GET /trading/signals` and
   `GET /trading/pairs`, two in-memory reads. Falsifiable: the backend's request log above 5 req/s
   from the bot with no user waiting.
8. **The first trade is the shown direction from the list or the card** (#829); the pressed
   second's reading (#828) serves a start without a record — the `n` door without an earlier draw,
   an old button, a bot restart — and about three such starts in four begin «📡 Ждём сигнал…».
   Falsifiable: after a quiet hour, `start_signal_action` NULL on more than 15 % of the sessions
   started from the list or the card (step 2's SQL, `shown_from` NULL).
9. **No age cap on a shown direction** (#829, decision 5): a direction shown long before the press
   is traded as shown, without a submit window. Falsifiable by the owner's reading of
   `shown_age_ms` and `first_intent_after_ms` (above 3 000 on shown starts) in step 2.
10. **A shown start inside the worker's socket-open window goes over REST** (#828's finding, kept
   by #829): the worker opens an account's socket on its next `SESSION_TICK_MS` (5 s) tick after the
   account entered work, plus the connect; a first intent created before that goes `rest_fallback`
   and its result comes with the catch-up (~15 s). The backend cannot see a ready socket, and a
   wait would spend the entry. Falsifiable: `first_transport = 'rest_fallback'` on more than 10 % of
   the sessions in step 2's SQL; then a follow-up issue.
